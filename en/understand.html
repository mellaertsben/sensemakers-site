After this module, participants can

1Explain in plain language how AI works: what it learned, from whom, and why it sometimes guesses
2Spot when an answer only sounds right, and check it
3Judge what happens to their data once they type something into a tool
4Ask three questions before trusting any AI answer

How the half-day runs

This is the program as we run it, shown here as a morning. Every part starts with something to do. We tune the examples to your sector.

  1. 09:00

    Who actually makes AI?

    DoHands up: who ever clicked on traffic lights to prove they're not a robot? Then you helped train AI.

    Take home“AI is made by people, and everyone here has already helped.”

  2. 09:15

    How does AI read your question?

    DoYou vote: is this image real or made by AI? Then you watch your own sentence fall apart into pieces and numbers, live.

    Take home“AI doesn't read your words; it calculates with numbers that stand for them.”

  3. 09:40

    How does a model learn?

    DoThe group trains a model itself, with pens and phones. Then it turns out it learned something other than what you meant.

    Take home“A model learns what separates the examples, not what you meant.”

  4. 10:20
    Break
  5. 10:35

    What is inside such a model?

    DoThree volunteers play one neuron together. Then you watch a network learn, live.

    Take home“A neural network is billions of dials, turned until its answers fit the examples.”

  6. 11:05

    Why does a wrong answer still sound right?

    DoYou play the language model yourself and guess the next word. With the story of a newspaper's summer reading list, full of books that don't exist.

    Take home“It writes what is likely, and likely is not the same as true.”

  7. 11:40
    Break
  8. 11:50

    Why does AI agree with you so easily?

    DoYou rate five pairs of answers. In three of them, the friendlier answer turns out to be the worse one.

    Take home“Its politeness and its refusals are trained habits, and both can bend.”

  9. 12:15

    Where does your question go?

    DoYou estimate what one question costs in power and water, and follow the route your data takes.

    Take home“Every prompt is a trip to someone else's computer, and the trip has a cost.”

  10. 12:30

    When do you trust an answer?

    DoYou apply three questions to a real case, and write on your card the one sentence you'll tell someone tonight.

    Take home“Ask what it's working with, what backs it up, and what happens if it's wrong.”

  11. 13:00
    End

What you leave with

Every participant goes home with something in hand. HR or L&D gets a report of the session.

  • A take-home card with six sentences and three questionsThe day's insights in one sentence each, such as “Likely is not the same as true.”
  • A workbookFour pages participants fill in themselves during the session, ending with their own next step.
  • The slides with all sourcesThe day after, as a pdf, with the source for every figure and every example.
  • A certificate of attendance, by nameFor every participant. It confirms attendance; it is not an exam.
  • For HR or L&D: a session reportAttendance, the evaluations and our debrief. Useful for your AI Act file.
