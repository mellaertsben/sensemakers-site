Think

Better decisions, fewer costly mistakes.

Critical thinking, before and after you prompt. AI gave everyone an analyst's tools, but not the training. In half a day your people learn to frame their question, weigh an answer and spot their own thinking errors.

  • Before you prompt: frame the question yourself
  • After it answers: weigh the source, the evidence and what is at stake
  • Practice on a real decision from your own work

After this module, participants can

1Ask a question so that AI thinks along, instead of telling them what they want to hear
2Weigh an answer: where does it come from, can I check it, what is at stake?
3Spot bias and thinking errors, in AI and in themselves, with a counter-move for each pitfall
4Judge how hard to check something: from a quick look to a full check

How the half-day runs

This is the program as we run it, shown here as a morning. Every part starts with something to do. In the last part, everyone works on a decision from their own work.

  1. 09:00

    Who made the mistake here?

    DoIn pairs you take apart four real AI blunders, such as the chatbot that agreed to sell a car for one dollar. Was it the machine, the person who set it up, or the person who used the result?

    Take home“The mistake is rarely in the machine alone.”

  2. 09:30

    How does your question steer the answer?

    DoYou ask the same question twice: why was this project a success, and why was it a failure? You are proven right both times. Then you place six situations on the doubt dial: what gets a quick look, what gets a full check?

    Take home“Your question is your first bias.”

  3. 10:15

    It sounds right. Is it?

    DoYou estimate first and only then ask AI, and you see how a first number pulls your judgment. Then bias bingo: eight pitfalls, twelve situations from work and from home.

    Take home“Know your pitfall, and your counter-move.”

  4. 11:00
    Break
  5. 11:30

    How do you decide with AI without handing over the thinking?

    DoYou work out a decision that really has to be made this month: first on your own, then with AI, then on your own again. A colleague looks for what the answer left out.

    Take home“First you, then AI, then you.”

  6. 12:30

    What will you do differently on Monday?

    DoYou sort situations into green, orange or red, and fill in your own checklist and Monday card: one task, one check rule, one question for your team.

    Take home“Whose data, who gets hurt, who answers for it?”

  7. 13:00
    End

What you leave with

Every participant goes home with something in hand. HR or L&D gets a report of the session.

  • A card with seven questionsThree before you ask AI, three for after it answers, and one for when there is no time.
  • A workbook with one real decisionWorked out before and after AI. You see in black and white what AI changed in your judgment.
  • Your own checklist and Monday cardThree check rules in your own words, and one task you tackle on Monday.
  • The slides with all sources, and a certificate by nameThe slides follow the day after, as a pdf. The certificate confirms attendance; it is not an exam.
  • For HR or L&D: a session reportAttendance, the evaluations and our debrief.
