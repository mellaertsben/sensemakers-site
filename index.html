Start where it hurts.

Most organisations pass through the same stages, in roughly the same order. Pick the one that sounds like you. We guide you along the way, wherever you join it.

"We need an AI strategy and a roadmap, and the landscape changes every month."

"Everyone is talking about it. Nobody owns it."

"We get impressive demos and can't tell what's real."

What usually needs to happen

Get the leadership team to the same picture, then decide where AI creates value for you and what to leave alone. Name an owner, pick one team to start with, and agree how you'll measure it before anyone builds anything.

Executive Briefing · two hours to three decisions The Scan · three weeks to a plan Or a Leadership Sprint

"We've rolled out the tools. Not much has actually changed."

"A handful of people are flying. The rest opened it once."

"We want our people better at working with AI."

What usually needs to happen

Give everyone the basics before the next licence, then build skills on people's own tasks, role by role. Put champions in every team, measure adoption with a real denominator, and change the way of working, not just the tooling.

AI Foundations · one day, everyone AI for your role Or a lead on your side of the table

"AI is everywhere in our company, and nobody really owns it."

"We're paying for three AI stacks and using one and a half."

"Two teams built the same agent and didn't know about each other."

What usually needs to happen

One register of every tool and agent: owner, cost, kill switch. A usage policy people can actually name, and the literacy to follow it, so shadow AI has nowhere left to hide.

The Audit · every tool with an owner AI Foundations Or the Programme

Turn what works into playbooks and champions, so the fifth team moves faster than the first. And keep a radar on the frontier, so the next model release is a decision, not a surprise.

Why now

The technology is no longer the bottleneck. The organisation is.

Generative AI put building and using AI within reach of every function. The tools arrived faster than the ownership, the skills and the habits to use them well. That gap is where the value gets lost, and it is where we work.

How it fits together

Start with a briefing. End with a radar.

Most organisations run it in this order. None of them has to: every format stands on its own, and the Academy runs alongside from the first week.

1 · Two hours

Executive Briefing

The state of AI for your industry and your organisation, and the three decisions your leadership team has to take.

2 · Six weeks

Leadership Sprint

Your leadership team makes its own plan: where the value is, what capability it takes, what you own. A 90-day plan per leader.

3 · Six to twelve months

Programme

A lead on your side runs the plan, with Foundations, role tracks, a Build Day and champions activated as the plan needs them.

4 · Ongoing

Frontier Watch

A radar on the frontier, a line to us, and a quarterly decision session, so the next shift is a decision rather than a surprise.

Frontier Watch · by subscription

Your AI radar and sounding board.

New models, new tools, new rules, every month. Nobody in your organisation has the time to follow it all, and the decisions get taken anyway. Frontier Watch is someone who follows the frontier for you, translates it to your stack, your roadmap and your policy, and is there when a choice comes up.

Frontier Watch, in full
Every monthA 90-minute briefing with your leadership team or AI lead, prepared with a note on what changed and what it means for you.
Whenever it comes upA standing line to us for tooling, adoption and policy questions, with a straight answer.
Every quarterA decision session with your board and an updated tooling and capability roadmap.
What we stand for

Five values you can hold us to.

Judgment stays sovereign

Always a stance

Measured in impact

Baselines from day one. We track value, not activity, and we tell you when the number didn't move.

At the frontier
