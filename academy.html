Lead
For a leadership team, or a keynote

Executive Briefing

Two hours, with a 45-minute intake before
Five to fifteen people: your leadership team
Tailored to your industry and your organisation

The state of AI, applied. What changed and what is noise; what your sector is doing with it, where the value shows up and where it doesn't; and then the part that matters: what it means for this organisation. The last hour is a working session on the three decisions your team has to take, on tooling, capability and ownership. Industry editions start where we know the ground, media and publishing and telecom, with others on request.

  • The frontier: the last few months, read for leaders, with a stance.
  • The industry: what peers and competitors are doing, and what to learn from it.
  • The organisation: a guided working session on your three decisions.

You leave with: one page with the decisions and the open questions, as input for a Leadership Sprint, a Scan or Frontier Watch.

For your leadership team, in-company

Leadership Sprint

Three half-days over six weeks
Six to ten leaders: your leadership team, or the leads of one business unit
Not a course. A working format

Your leadership team makes its own AI transformation plan, with us as sparring partner and the frameworks on the table. Three sessions, three decisions. Where the value is: use case discovery and prioritisation, with the workflow as the unit of analysis. What capability it takes: literacy, judgment, champions, and what that asks of your managers. What you own and what you rent: tooling strategy, AI spend and ownership, and the light governance that makes adoption safe.

  • Session one, where the value is: a prioritised shortlist.
  • Session two, which capability: the capability plan, mapped to the Academy formats.
  • Session three, what you own: the plan, and a 90-day plan per leader.

You leave with: the transformation plan on one page, an owner per decision, and a 90-day plan for every participant. The Programme picks it up from there, if you want it to.

Why not an open programme with peers from other companies? Because you would be buying knowledge and a network. We would rather come to your team and leave with your plan.

Work
For everyone in the organisation

AI Foundations

One day: understand it in the morning, use it in the afternoon
Eight to twenty people per session
Covers the AI literacy the EU AI Act asks of employers

The base every employee needs, in one day. The morning is literacy: how these models work, in plain language; what they can and can't do; hallucinations and how to check; data and confidentiality; when not to use it; and your own usage policy, applied. The afternoon is hands-on personal productivity in the tool your people already have: mail, meetings, writing, research, analysis, and the step from loose prompts to reusable assistants. Literacy without practice doesn't stick. Practice without literacy gives you shadow AI.

  • Morning, understand: how it works, where it fails, what never goes in, when to leave it.
  • Afternoon, use: your own tasks, done differently, in your own tool.
  • An attestation per participant, and for the organisation the proof of literacy the AI Act asks for. A digital edition follows in 2027.

You leave with: an organisation where everyone uses AI safely and usefully, a personal set of assistants per participant, and the paperwork for Article 4.

For analysts, advisers and the people who decide

Thinking with AI

One day, or two half-days with a real decision in between
Eight to sixteen people
Judgment in the age of assistants

AI moves critical thinking upstream: into the question, the framing, the assumptions, the evidence and the context of the decision. It also brings new ways to get it wrong, and they rarely announce themselves: agreeing with you too readily, trusting the output because it came from a machine, offloading the thinking altogether. This day is about deciding better with AI than without it. A decision process as the spine, the thinking tools that go with each step, and the new failure modes of working with AI, labelled by what the research supports and what is our framing.

  • A six-step decision process, with the tools that go with each step.
  • The new failure modes of working with AI, and how to catch them in your own work.
  • Practice on the participants' own decisions.

You leave with: a personal decision scorecard, a checklist of AI thinking mistakes for your own work, and the habit of checking before you act.

For one team, in one role

AI for your role

Half a day to a day, on site
One team, eight to sixteen people
Launch tracks: sales, marketing, software delivery

One team, its real workflows. We map where AI helps and where it doesn't, then work hands-on in the lab on the team's own tasks, with the quality and risk questions that belong to the role: customer data, compliance, tone. The team decides where AI sits in its work, who checks what, and writes it down. Launch tracks: AI for Sales, AI for Marketing, and AI in the Software Delivery Lifecycle for product and engineering teams, in Codex, Cursor or Claude Code. Other roles on request.

  • The team's workflows, mapped: where AI helps, where it doesn't, and the check for each.
  • Hands-on in the team's own tool, on tasks brought that morning.
  • Quality and risk specific to the role.

You leave with: a role playbook the team keeps and a set of assistants per workflow.

For cross-functional teams, on your own use cases

AI Build Day

One day, plus two to three weeks of preparation
Twenty to forty people in teams of four or five
In your own tools, no code. From 2027

Not a course about building. A day of building. Teams take real problems from your organisation and turn them into working prototypes, in the tools you already have, without code. The morning opens with a 90-minute builder crash course; the rest is building, with us as coaches, and a pitch to a jury from your leadership. The preparation is the real work: challenges taken in, ideally from the shortlist of a Leadership Sprint; tool access and sandbox data arranged; guardrails agreed. And the ending is not the pitch but the ownership: every prototype gets an owner and a 30-day decision. Continue, hand over to a build partner, or stop. Prototypes nobody owns die within a month.

  • Preparation: the challenges, the access, the guardrails, the teams.
  • The day: crash course, build, pitch.
  • The close: an owner and a 30-day decision for every prototype.

You leave with: five to eight working prototypes, a prioritised list of use cases with owners, and your first candidate champions.

For the organisation, over time

Frontier Watch

By subscription, annual, billed monthly
For CEOs, CIOs and AI leads
A retainer for decisions, not a newsletter

Frontier Watch, in full

Most of what changes in AI each month doesn't matter to you. Some of it matters a lot, and it rarely announces itself. Frontier Watch is someone who follows the frontier for you, translates it to your stack, your roadmap and your policy, and is there when a choice comes up. A monthly briefing with your leadership team or AI lead, a standing line to us in between, a quarterly decision session with your board, and once a year, refreshed lab material for the formats you have run.

  • Every month: a 90-minute briefing, prepared with a note on what changed and what it means for you.
  • In between: the line to us, for tooling, adoption and policy questions.
  • Every quarter: a decision session and an updated tooling and capability roadmap.

You keep: a roadmap that stays current, decisions taken on time rather than by surprise, and a leadership team that hears about the next shift before it hits.

For your internal enablers

AI Champions Programme

Two days, plus six monthly sessions
Six to twelve champions from one organisation
Usually part of a Programme. From 2027

We train twelve of your people. They train a thousand. Internal enablers, ambassadors, L&D, power users, get the AI Foundations material under licence and learn to run it themselves: giving the sessions, coaching colleagues, collecting use cases and feeding them back to leadership, dealing with resistance. Then six months of community of practice with us, with a shared library and new cases every month.

  • Two days: run Foundations yourselves, coach, collect, report.
  • Six months: a monthly session with us, a shared library, the next cases.
  • A licence on the materials, so it keeps running without us.

You keep: certified champions, the licence, an internal library of use cases, and a capability that lives in the organisation rather than in a supplier.

Two routes

Where most organisations start, and where they end up.

Every format stands on its own. These are the two routes we see most often.

Larger organisation

Briefing, sprint, programme, radar.

An Executive Briefing to get the leadership team to one picture. A Leadership Sprint to make the plan. The Programme to run it: Foundations for everyone through champions, an AI Build Day on the shortlist, role tracks per team, Thinking with AI for the people who decide. Then Frontier Watch, so it keeps going.

Smaller organisation

Foundations, one track, radar.

An Executive Briefing as a kick-off with the owner. AI Foundations for everyone, in one day. Then one role track for the team where it matters most, or Thinking with AI for the people who decide. Then Frontier Watch, so nobody has to follow the frontier on the side.
